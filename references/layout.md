# Layout: figures, diagrams and the build

Read when cropping figures, drawing diagrams or building.

## Layout

- The title in `guide.json` is the paper's own title followed by " – Distillation", with nothing before it. The footer starts "Distillation of", then the authors and the arXiv version.
- **The reader does not have the paper open.** So every figure and table that the guide cites appears in the guide, at the first place the text cites it, with its caption and its reading card. Every later mention links back to it. A claim-evidence table that cites Table 1 and Figure 4 is followed by both, before the next subsection. A sentence that cites a figure the guide does not show is a defect.
- Every image of a figure or table is a crop of the paper, or for a web article the article's own image file (`references/web.md`). Its caption is copied word for word and starts with its label and page marker: `<b>Figure 3.1 [p. 11]:</b>`, which prints as "Figure 3.1:".
- Draw diagrams in Mermaid: write the source in `WORK/diagrams/NAME.mmd` and render it with `scripts/diagram.py` to SVG, which stays sharp in the PDF.
- **The overview diagram** (`WORK/diagrams/gist.mmd`) opens Part 1, before its first subsection. It shows what the paper changes, so the reader knows what the dense sections will build. Draw two lanes that match step by step: the earlier approach this paper changes, and this paper's approach, each ending on its result. Put the lanes side by side as columns when rows would print too small. Colour the changed steps of the earlier lane amber, a problem it has red, and the new steps of this paper's lane blue. A step the paper keeps stays grey in both lanes.
- Three things make an overview work. First, the earlier lane is the one model the paper compares itself with, drawn box by box at the same level of detail as this paper's lane, so each amber box sits beside the blue box that changes it. Second, the diagram shows what happens for each output token, with the loop back for the next one. Third, it shows the paper's design options as branches, such as two kinds of attention or several score functions. The earlier lane can be only as detailed as the paper's own description of it: when the paper describes it briefly, draw fewer boxes rather than add details from elsewhere.
- **An intuition diagram** (`WORK/diagrams/intuition-NAME.mmd`) comes before the math of each critical section that the plan names. It shows the section's mechanism in a few steps, so the reader knows what each equation computes before reading it. When a paper figure already shows the same steps as operations, the diagram says in plain terms what each step does. Skip it only where the paper's figure already does that.
- **An architecture diagram** (`WORK/diagrams/architecture.mmd`) shows the parts of a system or model and how data moves between them. Draw one in systems mode, and for a method made of parts that work together rather than one chain of steps. It follows the overview in Part 1, or opens the part that explains the architecture; budget its space in the plan. Put the paper's own parts inside one dotted subgraph named after the method, with `class METHOD boundary`, and give each part class `part`. Give what sits outside the method class `ext`: the input, the output, a dataset, a pretrained model, an outside service. Draw stored data as a cylinder `ID[("Label")]`, parallel copies as `ID@{ shape: procs, label: "Label" }`, and an optional or occasional link as a dotted arrow. Each label is the paper's own name for the part, one to three words in Title Case. `diagram.py` supplies the colours for these classes, and for the overview's `kept`, `changed`, `problem` and `new`, so a diagram names the class and never restates its colours.
- **A worked-example diagram** (`WORK/diagrams/example-NAME.mmd`) may follow the math of a critical section. It traces the running example with its real numbers in plain-word labels ("1 + 0.00000003 is stored as 1.0"), so the reader sees the values move. Numbers are allowed; symbols, formulas and section labels are not.
- **A chart drawn by the guide's own code** may sit beside the paper's figure when it shows the mechanism at a smaller or simpler scale: a zoom, a toy run, or a function the paper only describes. Its numbers come from a numbered Code snippet printed in the guide, and its caption names that snippet and says what is simplified. Size its text to print at 7 pt or more.
- The overview and intuition diagrams use terms only: each label is a term with a few plain words. An image cannot carry links, and the math follows in the text, so a diagram has no symbols, formulas, or Part, Math, section or figure labels. Its caption names the pages it is drawn from.
- Draw only what the paper says. The earlier approach's lane comes from this paper's own description of it, or from a cited work that was read, never from other sources.
- Labels print near body size: `diagram.py` fails below 7 pt at full column width. Raise "fontSize" in the file's `%%{init}%%` line, or use fewer boxes per row, short labels and tight spacing. Keep an intuition diagram under about 100 pt tall, and budget every diagram's space in the plan.
- **Shape a diagram to the page, not the page to the diagram.** These are defaults; change them when the rendered page shows a better fit.
  - A chain of steps reads best as one horizontal row (`flowchart LR`) with two-line labels joined by `<br/>`. A top-down chain of five boxes fills half a page.
  - A two-lane overview reads best as two horizontal lanes stacked one above the other: an outer `flowchart TB`, each lane a subgraph with `direction LR`, and `LaneA ~~~ LaneB` to fix their order. Two vertical lanes inside an outer `flowchart LR` can render stacked instead of side by side, and then fill a page.
  - When `diagram.py` reports labels under 7 pt, first tighten the spacing (`%%{init: {"flowchart": {"nodeSpacing": 14, "rankSpacing": 22, "padding": 6}}}%%`), then raise `fontSize` in the same line, then shorten labels or cut boxes.
- **Choose each image's width by its shape.** A diagram, a table with many columns, or a figure with several panels side by side goes in `snap wide`: full column width, with its card below. A tall, narrow figure keeps the default `snap`, with its card beside it. A wide image squeezed into the half-width `snap` column prints too small to read.
- **Start a part on a new page only when the page before it ends near the bottom.** `build.py` fails on a page more than half empty, and a forced break after a short page is the usual cause. Look at the spreads before you add an id to `new_page_sections`.
- **Name the guide's own sections without the word "Section".** Write "the next section" or "2.2", because `audit_links.py` reads "Section 2.2" as a heading in the paper.
- Keep each panel in reading order. Mermaid reorders boxes that share the same links, so put a set of parallel items in one box ("x1 · x2 · x3").
- A diagram's caption says what it shows and where in the paper it is drawn from, with no "Figure" label, so it is never taken for the paper's figure: "Recurrence against self-attention, drawn from Section 1 and Table 1 [pp. 2, 6]".
- List the metric section in `new_page_sections` and in `compact_sections`.
- A formula must fit its card. Split a long formula over two lines, because one wide element shrinks the whole PDF.
- Long tables may break between rows. Keep a heading with the content after it.
